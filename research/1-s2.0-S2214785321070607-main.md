Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) is a method of polymer 3D printing. Since the material is laid layer by
layer, the printing process parameters will affect the performance of the printed part mainly due to bond-
ing between the layers and print quality. This paper studies the effect of various printing parameters
(Layer thickness, build orientation and raster angle) on the mechanical properties of PLA specimens pro-
cessed and tested as per ASTM standard through Design of Experiment (DOE) using Taguchi approach by
L9 orthogonal array. Printed specimens were tested using tensometer under normal conditions and raw
data obtained were used to evaluate the inﬂuence of each process parameter on percentage elongation
and Ultimate Tensile Strength (UTS). It is observed that layer thickness has negative correlation with
UTS. Mean UTS tend to decrease with increase in layer thickness. Specimen tend to show maximum ten-
sile strength at 450 build orientation and raster angle has less signiﬁcant on tensile strength compare to
other two selected process parameter for the current study. Maximum UTS observed is 46.65 MPa for
0.1 mm layer thickness, 300 raster angle and 00 build orientation. To assess if process variables have
any signiﬁcant features, the analysis of variance (ANOVA) is performed. It is observed that layer thickness
has more inﬂuence than build orientation and raster angle.

1. Introduction
3D printing also known as additive manufacturing it produces a
part by adding materials in a layer wise fashion unlike the tradi-
tional or CNC machining processes which remove materials from
a bulk material, hence known as subtractive manufacturing. Due
to this 3D printing produces minimal or no material waste except
for the support structure in case of intricate shapes [1,2]. There are
many ways of 3D printing the product out of which Fused Deposi-
tion Modeling (FDM) is extensively used 3D printing techniques in
which fused polymer is laid layer by layer on a platform [3]. Due to
which the mechanical properties of the ﬁnal product depends on
the printing parameters such as orientation, raster angle, deposi-
tion rate, layer thickness, inﬁll density etc. [5–11]. Since the parts
with high complex shapes can be produced using FDM [9], its
strength to withstand the load under different application is most
important. Since parts are printed here is by adding one layer
above the other ﬁnal strength of the parts depends on the bonding
between each layers. This adhesion or bonding between the layers
majorly depends on temperature at which each layers will come in
contact together at the time of welding. And orientation of the
printing parts through FDM also places major role in withstanding
the load. FDM method mostly uses thermoplastic polymers such as
Poly Lactic Acid (PLA), Poly Carbonate (PC), Acrylonitrile Butadiene
Styrene (ABS), Poly-Ether-Ether-Ketone (PEEK) etc. Strength of the
FDM part can also be increased by using ﬁlaments with CF infused
in base material [14]. Using composite ﬁlaments can be a possible
substitute for expensive metallic parts made using titanium [9].
It has been observed that, for monotonic loads, if the printing
parameters are such that load is applied perpendicular to the layer,
the mechanical properties are lower as compared to, parallel to the

tonic tension and ﬂexure strength [8]. Raster angle also affects
the strength of the part. For a monotonic tension, 0 and 90 degree
raster angles showed highest and lowest strength respectively
[16]. Usually, for metal, fatigue life is affected mostly by mean
stress, surface factors such as toughness, ﬁnish, treatment etc.
[17]. For FDM polymer part, along with the factors mentioned
above, printing parameters also affect the fatigue life [12,13,18–
21]. Even though the material used in FDM might be isotropic,
net property of the part will be anisotropic which maybe caused
due to weaker layer adhesion, layer porosity and phase transition
when the fused polymer solidiﬁes [6,7]. Instead of modeling the
part as anisotropic, success has been seen when the material is
modeled as transversely isotropic [15]. Process parameters not
only affect the mechanical properties but also the net cost [10].
Longer printing time, high inﬁll density and lower layer height
implies greater cost.
From the literature it is observed that work has been carried out
towards process parameter optimization for FDM printer with dif-
ferent materials. But many researchers have been focused towards
optimizing inﬁll pattern and temperature of nozzle in FDM for dif-
ferent material without much emphasis on build orientation of
specimens. It is also reported in the literature that printing at ele-
vated temperature has wide effect on crystalline and bonding
properties of the each layer in the specimen enhancing its mechan-
ical strengths. Even different combination of process parameters
has signiﬁcant impact on mechanical strength of the printed spec-
imens which is not discussed in the literature. Build orientation
places important role when linear line inﬁll pattern is chosen
which is not discussed in relation to layer thickness and raster
angle in the literature. Hence in the current study with build orien-
tation, raster angle and layer thickness combination process
parameters is selected to investigated its individual and combina-
tion effect on mechanical strength of the printed part at optimum
printing temperature and print speed using Taguchi approach.
2. Material and method
Polylactic Acid is biodegradable and hence referred to as bio-
plastic or thermoplastic-starch. It has characteristics similar to
polypropylene (PP), polyethylene (PE), and Polystyrene (PS) and
can be produced from already existing manufacturing equipment
which makes it relatively cost efﬁcient to produce. Some of the
characteristics of bulk PLA are given in Table 1. Since PLA is
biodegradable and sustainable thermoplastic alternative wastage
can be minimized from the end used manufacturing components.
Addition to this even though PLA as more strength compare to
ABS and possess similar properties and characteristics as ABS, its
ductility will be less. Due to its biocompatibility property PLA parts
manufactured using FDM process ﬁnds its importance in tissue
engineering, medical ﬁeld where parts will made as per patient
needs [22,23]. There are a vast array of applications for Polylactic
Acid like plastic ﬁlms and biodegradable tools (e.g. screws, pins,
rods, and plates etc.). It is the default ﬁlament choice for most
extrusion-based 3D printers because it can be printed at a low tem-
perature, complete control environment is not required like ABS
where warpage will be main problem when printing in open
atmosphere.

2.2. Optimizing the printing parameter using Taguchi
There are several printing parameters which effect the strength
and mechanical properties of 3D printed specimens as observed in
literature viz., Inﬁll density, inﬁll pattern, raster angle, printing
speed, layer thickness, build orientation, printing temperature,
build plate temperature and type of material used for printing.
Not much observation has been made from literature for PLA on
effect of build orientation, raster angle and layer thickness on
mechanical strength and it is reported that with increase in inﬁll
density mechanical strength increases [5] and with varying build
orientation specimens, build along longitudinal axis inﬁll pattern
direction possess maximum tensile [10,11] but no work has been
noticed on effect of build orientation along Z axis. And in this work
efforts has been made to choose three printing parameter viz. ras-
ter angle build orientation and layer thickness with three levels
with constant print temperature and print speed. The research
has been initiated with Taguchi Design of Experiment (DOE) by

5. Conclusion
In order with applications of 3D printing, it is necessary to vary
mechanical conﬁgurations of 3D printed parts in order to provide
mechanical strength and stiffness or regulated weight. Numerous
printing combinations add up to ﬂexibility in terms of time con-
sumption to manufacture, post process or required effective
strength and stiffness. In this paper, efforts have made to deter-
mined effects of raster angle, build orientation and layer thickness
on the UTS of 3D-printed PLA specimens. From the experiment and
ANOVA it is concluded that, with layer thickness and raster angle
has negative correlation when combined with build orientation.
Maximum UTS is observed at 450 build orientation of the three
level 0, 45 and 900 considered for the current study.
From ANOVA it conceals that contribution of layer thickness is
maximum compare to other two process parameter. And raster
angle has least signiﬁcance with contribution less than 1%.From
the experimental maximum UTS 46.61 MPa is observed for
0.1 mm layer thickness, 300 raster angle and 450 build orientation.
Further the work can be extended to ﬁnd the effect of other process
parameters and its interaction on different mechanical and tribo-
logical properties of various materials.
