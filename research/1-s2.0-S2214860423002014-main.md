A B S T R A C T
With the rapid evolution and application of 3D printing technology, production capacity can be easily expanded
by increasing the number of 3D printing machines or implementing distributed production to meet customer
demand. According to our best knowledge, no paper has been found to address the process stability and pro­
duction efficiency of 3D printers used in mass production or distributed manufacturing. This paper takes the
material extrusion (MEX) 3D printing process as an example, aiming to minimize the difference in the quality of
parts fabricated by different printers or nozzles at a lower printing cost by selecting the appropriate process
parameter settings. First, combine the latent variable Gaussian process (LVGP) model with the split-plot
experimental design to investigate the mechanical properties and costs of printing parts in relation to critical
process parameters. Second, under consideration of the in-process uncertainty, the interval number theory is
used to measure the split-plot effect of the mechanical properties of printing parts. Finally, a multi-objective
optimization model integrating the split-plot effect and printing cost is established to get the Pareto optimal
solution. The grey relational analysis (GRA) is used to select the optimal compromise solutions from the Pareto
solution sets. The results of the validation experiments show that a more efficient and stable printing process can
be achieved under the optimal parameter settings obtained by the proposed approach. This study will provide
theoretical references and technical support for the standardization and industrialization of 3D printing
technology.
1. Introduction
Additive manufacturing (AM) is the process of joining materials to
make parts from 3-dimensional (3D) model data (ISO/ASTM 52900:
2021) [1,2]. Material extrusion (MEX) is a widely used AM technology
due to its design space, production flexibility, and low labor input.
Polylactic acid (PLA) parts printed by MEX technology have been
extensively utilized in various industries, such as aerospace, automotive,
biomedical, smart home, and creative gifts [3–5]. However, to shorten
the production lead time or meet the fluctuating market demands,
production capacity should be easily expanded by increasing the num­
ber of 3D printing machines or implementing distributed production
[6–8]. It is worth noting that the variability of 3D printing processes can
lead to inconsistent quality and performance of printing parts, which
prevents its application in mass production. To identify the sources of
process variability, Kechagias, et al. [9] classify 3D printing process
parameters into three categories: signal, control, and noise parameters,
according to robust parameter design theory. The effect of the variability
of each parameter on the surface quality (SQ) and dimensional accuracy
(DA) of parts is summarized through an extensive literature review. In
the meantime, Kechagias, et al. [10] use the Taguchi L25 orthogonal
array experimental design to investigate the importance of the vari­
ability of process parameters, such as infill density, nozzle temperature,
and print speed, in achieving specific mechanical strength of FFF parts.
They point out that the selection of 3D printing equipment and 3D
printing process parameters will affect the final performance of the
parts. Yang, et al. [11] show that even under the same parameter setting,
the performance of multiple parts printed continuously with the same

critical for AM technology toward standardization and industrialization

The setting of MEX process parameters will directly affect the quality
and costs of printing parts. Therefore, choosing the appropriate
parameter settings is essential for improving the stability and production
efficiency of the MEX process. To identify and optimize the parameters
that significantly affect the MEX process, Wang, et al. [13] combine
Taguchi’s method with gray relational analysis to investigate the effect
of process parameters, such as layer thickness, deposition mode, depo­
sition direction, and support mode, on the surface roughness of parts.
Nancharaiah [14] uses Taguchi’s L9 orthogonal array and variance
analysis techniques to study the relationship between critical process
parameters and printing time. The author points out that air gap and
layer thickness greatly influence the printing time and gives their
optimal parameter design values. In addition to printing quality and
costs, Kechagias and Chaidas [15] analyze the sustainability of 3D
printing processes from energy consumption and its impact on society.
They point out that lower CO2 emissions and energy consumption parts
can be achieved through parameter optimization. However, due to the
influence of process noise, there is often a highly complex nonlinear
relationship between process parameters and part performances, which
makes it challenging to create an accurate mathematical model. To
overcome the limitations of linear regression models in nonlinear fitting,
Balkin and Lin [16] regard the manufacturing process as a "black box"
and combine artificial neural networks (ANN) with the design of ex­
periments to fit the model of the inputs and outputs. Vining and Bohn
[17] apply kernel regression (KR) to the modeling and optimization of
the 3D printing process. Besides KR, the Gaussian process (GP) can fit the
complex nonlinear relationship in the 3D printing process due to the
ability to approximate expensive and computationally intensive func­
tions using limited samples[18–21]. More introductions on existing
technologies and future research trends for optimizing the application
characteristics of MEX 3D printing PLA parts can be found in the liter­
ature[22–25].
Many scholars and practitioners have explored various experimental
designs, models, and optimization approaches to research the impact of
MEX process parameters on printing costs or part performance[26–30].
However, there are numerous MEX process parameter settings and
complex internal connection mechanisms. How to find a compromise for
process parameter settings according to the overall goal is the bottleneck
limiting the advancement of MEX 3D printing technology. For example,
increasing the filling density can improve the mechanical properties of
printing parts, e.g., compressive and tensile strength. However, as the
infill density increases, so does the printing time and the length of the
consumed PLA wire. On the other hand, increasing print speed can
reduce printing time and material costs. A fast print speed can result in
problems, such as collapse and drawing, as well as the surface quality
problem of printing parts. Likewise, reducing the layer thickness can
improve the surface quality of parts, but it results in a longer printing
time since the thin layer thickness requires more layers to be printed.
Therefore, to find a balance point between conflicting MEX process
parameter combinations, multi-objective optimization is required to
ensure the expected production targets[31–33].
Although many achievements have been obtained in MEX process
improvement, current research mainly focuses on improving the mold­
ing process for a particular nozzle of a single printer[34–38]. No detailed
study has been found to meaningfully address the stability and pro­
duction efficiency of the MEX process in mass production or distributed
manufacturing. Therefore, to fill this research gap, this paper aims to
develop a meta-modeling and robust optimization method to minimize
the variation in the quality of parts printed by different printers or
nozzles at a lower printing cost by selecting the appropriate process
parameter settings. First, two different 3D printers were selected as the
test beds. Each of these printers has two nozzles. The printers and noz­
zles are referred to as two equipment parameters in this paper, i.e.,
printers A vs. B and left nozzle vs. right nozzle. The six critical process
parameters were selected and adjusted during the experiments, i.e.,
nozzle temperature, platform temperature, infill density, layer thick­
ness, printing speed, and infill pattern. Based on the equipment available
in the laboratory, compression tests are carried out on printing parts
using a microcomputer-controlled electronic tensile testing machine.
The compression deformation, compressive strength, and printing time
of printing parts were selected as the output responses of interest.
Develop an experimental scheme using the split-plot and maximum
projection (MaxPro) design [39] for the experiments. Second, a new
multi-response latent variable Gaussian process (MR-LVGP) model is
developed to establish the complex nonlinear relationship between in­
puts and outputs. Under the in-process uncertainty, the interval number
theory measures the split-plot effect of the mechanical properties of
printing parts. Finally, considering the split-plot effect and printing cost,
a multi-objective optimization model is established to find the param­
eter setting that makes the MEX process more efficient and stable.

2. Experiments and measurements
2.1. Experimental materials and equipment
In this paper, PolyTerra PLA wire with a diameter of 1.75 mm from
Polymaker was selected as the printing material, and two dual-nozzle 3D
printers with a model of Flashforge@Creator 3 were used to print cube
sample parts the dimension of 20 mm × 20 mm× 20 mm. The printing
parts were subjected to compression tests using a microcomputer-
controlled electronic tensile testing machine, and the mechanical
properties of printing parts were collected online using the industrial
digital camera. The detailed description of the experimental equipment
is shown in Table 1, and the experimental equipment and the MEX
process schematic are presented in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2, respectively. It
should be pointed out that the red serial numbers marked on the
experimental equipment in Fig. 1 are consistent with the serial numbers
in Table 1.
2.2. Experimental design and sample preparation
In the 3D printing process, selecting and setting the relevant process
parameters will directly affect the mechanical properties and costs of
printing parts. To identify and optimize the parameters that significantly
affect the MEX process, we have studied a large amount of literature on
the optimization of MEX process parameters. In addition, under different
parameter settings, over 800 cube samples of 20 mm × 20 mm× 20 mm
were printed using the same equipment and PLA wire. The samples were
subjected to compression tests to gain an in-depth understanding of the
influence of the setting of various process parameters on the mechanical
properties and costs of the samples. Based on the literature analysis and
our practical experience, this paper selects six key process parameters:
layer thickness, printing speed, nozzle temperature, platform tempera­
ture, infill density, infill pattern, and two equipment parameters of
printer and nozzle as variable parameters. Other process parameters and
environmental parameters remain fixed. The specific ranges of values
for the variable parameters are shown in Table 2.
When printing samples using the Flashforge @ Creator 3 printer, it is
necessary to slice the computer-aided design (CAD) data file using
FlashPrint 4.3.0 software and then print it layer by layer. We can set the
values of process parameters, such as layer thickness, filling density, and
printing speed, when using FlashPrint 4.3.0 software to slice digital files.
The standard nozzle diameter of the Flashforge @ Creator 3 printer used
in this study is 0.4 mm, which means 0.4 mm is the maximum thickness
that can be set[32,40]. The printer used in this paper can print parts with
various layer thicknesses within a reasonable range of [0.05, 0.4] mm.
Traditional experimental design theory usually assumes that the
experimental scheme is entirely random. However, in our experiments,
there are two printers, A and B, each printer has two nozzles, and each
nozzle can choose to print PLA parts under different MEX process
parameter settings. Therefore, there is a correlation among the printers,
nozzles, and MEX process parameters, which confirms the view of Box
[41], i.e., it is generally impractical to achieve complete randomization
in the designs of industrial experiments. Furthermore, the split-plot
experimental design can provide an effective scheme for experiments
with correlation constraints between input factors. According to the
difficulty of adjusting the factor level, the printer, nozzle position, and
MEX process parameters are divided into primary, secondary, and ter­
tiary factors, respectively. First, the main plots are divided according to
the number of levels of the primary factors, and the level settings of the
primary factors are randomly arranged in the main plots. Second, each
main plot is divided into the same number of cells as the secondary
factor level settings, called split plots, and each level setting of the
secondary factor is introduced in the split plots. Finally, for the tertiary
factors, 18 level combinations are designed by MaxPro design for six
MEX process parameters x = {x1,x2,…,x6}that contain both qualitative
and quantitative data. The three-level processing defined by 18 MEX
process parameter level combinations is introduced in the split-split

to improve the stability of other feedstock 3D printing technology or
similar manufacturing processes.

●Currently, to our best knowledge, no detailed studies have compre­
hensively analyzed the process stability and production efficiency of
the MEX process in mass production and distributed manufacturing.
Therefore, this paper investigates the modeling and optimization
strategies between the stability and productivity of the MEX process
and key process parameters. This study provides an important idea
for the standardization and industrialization development of 3D
printing technology.
This paper mainly focuses on optimizing the process stability and
production efficiency for a single dual-nozzle printer or two identical
models of dual-nozzle printers. However, multiple printers are often
required to perform a production task simultaneously in mass produc­
tion or distributed manufacturing. Therefore, it is of future interest to
optimize the process stability and production efficiency for three or
more printers. In addition, the change process of compression defor­
mation with compression time deserves further study to grasp the failure
mechanism of parts in practical industrial applications.
