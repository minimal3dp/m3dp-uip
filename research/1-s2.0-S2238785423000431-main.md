a b s t r a c t
Carbon ﬁbers (CFs) were used as reinforcement in developing a polyethylene terephthalate
glycol (PETG)-based polymer composite using the fused deposition modeling (FDM) 3D
printing technique. The inﬂuence of CF and process factors (inﬁll percentage, layer thick-
ness, inﬁll pattern) were studied by measuring the prepared polymer composite's tensile,
ﬂexural, and compressive properties. The innovative work that was carried out for this
study and the tests that were performed revealed that it is difﬁcult to predict the position of
the specimen break area before a test. The PETG-reinforced polymer only showed
enhanced ﬂexural and tensile strength at a layer thickness of 0.25 mm and a maximum
inﬁll percentage of 20% for a solid structural design. Compressive strength improved in
reinforced PETG hexagonal and circle structures. The conﬁrmation of the numerical
modeling applied to determine the mechanical properties of PETG for FDM additive
manufacturing is one of the goals suggested in this research, along with a comparison of
experimental and computational data. Scanning electron microscopy examination of the
fractured sample surfaces revealed various fracture mechanisms and morphologies for the
materials tested. The research found that the 3D-printed composite could further expand
the application of PETG as an engineering material.

1.
Introduction
Today's manufacturing industry is signiﬁcantly reliant on
three-dimensional (3D) printing technologies, which demon-
strate sustainable and functional solutions in a highly
adaptable and cost-effective manner [1,2]. Geometrical and
surface deformities should be tackled precisely by creating
helpful hybrid systems that can be used during pre-handling,
on-printing, and post-handling [3]. According to the ﬁndings
of the study, the incorporation of (CF) at a weight of 20% as a
reinforcing agent in polyethylene terephthalate glycol (PETG)
produced a composite material that displayed improved ten-
sile strength ranging from a maximum of 114% for triangular
patterns to a minimum of 43.7% for full honeycomb patterns
[4]. It has been demonstrated that a composite structure

approach for fabricating 3D auxetic cellular structures, with

mens. Compression tests established the structures' auxetic
character.

2.5.
Finite element method
In addition to the experiments, simulations were carried out
better to understand the structural inﬂuence on the test re-
sults. In this implementation, a standard numerical approach
was employed [27]. While linear elements for movement were
utilized, stresses inside the elements were constant. ANSYS
13 was used for postprocessing, where the stresses were
softened due to the use of the same mesh for improved
viewing.
Different structure designs were used in numerical anal-
ysis inﬂuenced by slicer-induced anisotropy, and modeled the
material as homogeneous and isotropic (standard bond be-
tween layers and ﬁbers) using the same engineering con-
stants; Young's modulus, E was 5230 MPa, and Poisson's ratio,
v was 0.27. All pre-processing procedures, including CAD ge-
ometry, had a 2 mm element size and a total mesh count of
12220, with a target density of 1183 g/cm3. The linear form
functions and tetrahedral elements were employed for spatial
discretization with an h-convergence analysis-determined
mesh.

4.
Conclusions
The compressive strength of reinforced polyethylene tere-
phthalate glycol (PETG) polymer was shown to be enhanced,
but only at a layer thickness of 0.25 mm by a maximum of 20%
at 80% inﬁll for the hexagonal and circle structures. In the
cases of tensile and ﬂexural testing, the Scanning electron
microscopy (SEM) fracture surface examination of the sam-
ples revealed various fracture developments and morphol-
ogies for the materials tested. Additionally, various layer
processes were found for the composites on surfaces sub-
jected to compression testing. In addition, the function of (CF)
in smoothing the surfaces and altering the compression fail-
ure mode from delamination to ﬁber pull out was evident.
