The Additive Manufacturing technology initially developed as a visualization and validation tool. The
recent advancement in this technology like Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM), Stereolithography (SLA)
and Selective Laser Sintering (SLS) make it rapid manufacturing. However, the manufacturing of ready
to use parts using FDM is a challenging task. The processing parameters like inﬁll density, inﬁll pattern,
extrusion temperature affect the design, quality, functionality and mechanical properties of the ﬁnal part
vigorously. The present article discusses the effects of material density, inﬁll density and extrusion tem-
perature on tensile strength of Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene (ABS), Polyethylene Terephthalate Glycol
(PETG), and Multi-material test pieces. Multi-material is fabricated by merging 50% of ABS and 50% of
PETG layer by layer in FDM 3D Printing. A total of 30 test pieces as per the ASTM D638-(IV) standard
was printed having different inﬁll density, extrusion temperature, and material density. In addition to
this, Inﬁll density and extrusion temperature are optimized for increasing the tensile strength of FDM
fabricated units. Artiﬁcial neural network (ANN) and genetic algorithm-artiﬁcial neural network
(GA-ANN) hybrid tool in MATLAB-16.0 are used for training and optimization purposes. It is observed that
GA-ANN has maximized the tensile strength up to 4.54% and it has been validated experimentally.

1. Introduction
3D printing is an advanced manufacturing technology that fab-
ricates parts layer by layer deposition using the FDM 3D printer as
shown in Fig. 1. The amount of material is controlled by G-codes
using slicing software. FDM is one of the most important 3D print-
ing technology in which parts are build using thermoplastic ﬁla-
ments. The ﬁlaments are generally having a circular cross-section
with 1.75 mm diameters. FDM fabricates the consolidated parts
having complex shapes that reduce the number of assembly units
[1]. FDM reduces the lead time along with transportation and stor-
age which help in the supply chain [2]. In traditional manufactur-
ing, the part is shaped by either plastic deformation or subtractive
processes like turning, milling, grinding, etc. But rapid prototyping,
production is additive or generative. Fused Deposition Modeling
uses the polymer material to print the 3d layers of the product.
In FDM 3d printing ﬁlament is heated in the hot end in a semi-
liquid form that comes out through the nozzle (0.4 mm) and
ejected on the bedplate on the previous layer. The most commonly
used materials are Polylactic Acid (PLA), Nylon, PETG and ABS. The
International Organization for Standardization (ISO)/American
Society for Testing and Materials (ASTM) deﬁnes Additive manu-
facturing, informally known as three-dimensional printing, as
‘‘process of adding the material layer by layer to form 3D parts,
as a resist to traditional manufacturing and developmental manu-
facturing techniques” [3]. Diversity in medical instills from Com-
puted Tomography-imaged tendon replicas have the heedfulness
of those in the medical ﬁeld [4]. Not only in the medical ﬁeld,
but this technology is also effectively used in the construction ﬁeld.
A huddle of houses was printed in bulk quantity by WinSun in
China within 24 h [5]. This technology is getting the attention of
the automotive sector because of the intricate designs and person-
alization of certain car parts [6]. Fused deposition modeling is the
economical technique of additive manufacturing, which is used for
making the working prototypes of thermoplastic polymer/compos-
ites [7–12]. The vital drawback of this technology is the top layer
roughness, which relies upon the printer minimal thickness [13].

2. Methodology
The test pieces of different materials like ABS, PETG, and Multi-
material were designed in PTC Cero 1.0 software and fabricated
with the use of the FDM 3d printer. These test pieces were tested
on UTM for tensile strength and the essential process parameters
were optimized and validated using a hybrid GA-ANN tool.
2.1. Experimental work
ASTM D638 Type IV standards having thickness (T) 4 mm, a
radius of the outer part (RO) of the specimen is taken 25 mm,
and whereas the radius (R) of the curved part is taken as 14 mm.
The distance (D) between the grips is 65 mm with a gage length
(G) of the specimen is 25 mm and the overall length (LO) and the
overall width (WO) of the specimen are taken as 115 mm and
19 mm respectively which is shown in Fig. 2. Test pieces were pre-
pared in the PTC Cero software and converted into an STL ﬁle.
CURA 4.1.0 software, which is used for slicing the STL ﬁle and save
it into GCODE format. GCODE ﬁle fed to the 3d printing machine
with the help of either a memory card or a pen drive. For the fab-
rication of the workpiece, a 0.1 mm gap is maintained between the
nozzle and the build plate. The layer height (0.2 mm), wall thick-
ness (1.8 mm), inﬁll pattern (Grid) have been ﬁxed while printing.
For a smoother surface, we enable the ironing, in which nozzle
goes over the ﬁnal surface one more time without extruding the ﬁl-
ament. The printing speed was 70–80 mm/s for both the ﬁlaments.
The test piece for each material i.e. ABS, PTEG, and Multi-material
are fabricated by depositing material layer by layer. All the move-
ments are controlled by the various stepper motors as per the con-
trol program. For the manufacturing of a multi-material workpiece,
half part of the test piece is built by using ABS ﬁlament and the
other half is fabricated using PETG ﬁlament.
30 test pieces having magnitude 115 mm length, 19 mm width,
and 4 mm thickness was fabricated on the GEEETECH A30 fused
deposition modeling machine using ABS and PETG thermoplastic.

2.3. GA-ANN modeling
A basic genetic algorithm (GA) uses the random population
within the speciﬁed range of input parameters to evaluate the
given objective function. Further, the genetic algorithm incorpo-
rates the mutation and cross over function to produce new off-
spring for getting greater ﬁtness as elite. Using table 1 data, the
ANN model has been trained between input and output parame-
ters and further GA has been applied to maximize the output.
The input matrix (30  3) and output data (30  1) have been
imported in the neural ﬁtting tool as shown in Fig. 4. The tangent
sigmoid activation function has been used for each layer and feed-
forward backpropagation network has been created. 70% sample
has been used for training, 15% sample for testing and 15% sample
for cross-validation. Testing does not bear any impact on training.

4. Conclusions
This work investigates the inﬂuence of inﬁll density, material
density, and extrusion temperature on the mechanical properties
of FDM printed parts using different materials. The analysis shows
that the tensile strength is affected by extrusion temperature and
inﬁll density in an identical way. Tensile strength is more inﬂu-
enced by extrusion temperature rather than inﬁll density; higher
extrusion temperature favors the high tensile strength of the test
pieces up to a certain limit. The maximum tensile strength 44 N/
mm2 is observed in PETG material at an extrusion temperature of
225 C at 40% inﬁll density in experimental work. The hybrid
