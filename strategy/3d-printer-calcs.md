Unlike ringing (which decays), VFAs are constant and speed-dependent. They are caused by the stepper motor's internal resonance or the interaction between belt teeth and idlers (the polygon effect).7Theoretical FrameworkStepper motors operate in discrete steps. Even with microstepping, the torque ripple creates a vibration at the fundamental step frequency and its harmonics. Recommendation: Switch to toothed idlers or twist the belt.7Case B: If $f_{observed}$ matches the motor's natural frequency (or harmonic), the artifact is Motor Resonance. Stepper Motor Max Velocity (Back EMF) CalculatorProblem StatementThe trend of upgrading 3D printers to 48V power supplies is driven by the need to overcome Back EMF. However, users often guess their new speed limits. A physics-based calculator can determine the theoretical maximum velocity before torque collapse.11Theoretical FrameworkStepper motors generate a voltage (Back EMF) that opposes the driver's supply voltage. The rate at which current can rise in the coils is limited by inductance ($L$). As speed increases, the time available to inject current decreases, eventually starving the motor of torque.Mathematical ModelThe "Corner Speed" or maximum angular velocity ($\omega_{max}$) before significant torque loss can be estimated by:$$\text{Max Speed (rev/s)} \approx \frac{V_{supply}}{2 \cdot L \cdot I_{max} \cdot \text{StepsPerRev}}$$To convert this to linear printing speed ($V_{mm/s}$):$$V_{mm/s} = \text{Rev/s} \cdot (\text{Belt Pitch} \cdot \text{Pulley Teeth})$$Note: A safety factor (efficiency $\eta \approx 0.7$) should be applied to account for resistive losses and dynamic friction.13Detailed Formula Integration$$ V_{max_safe} = \left( \frac{V_{supply}}{2 \cdot L_{mH} \cdot 10^{-3} \cdot I_{peak} \cdot \text{SPR}} \right) \cdot (\text{Pitch} \cdot \text{Teeth}) \cdot 0.7 $$Inputs:Supply Voltage ($V$): 12, 24, or 48 Volts.Inductance ($L$): Value in milliHenry (mH) from motor datasheet.Current ($I$): Peak current setting (A).Steps per Revolution ($SPR$): 200 (1.8°) or 400 (0.9°).Mechanicals: Pulley Teeth (16/20).Output: Theoretical Max Velocity ($mm/s$).Application Logic & Strategic ValueThis calculator serves as a critical "sanity check" for users.Scenario: A user with high-inductance (4.5mH) motors on 24V tries to print at 500mm/s.Result: The calculator shows a max safe speed of 320mm/s.Action: The app warns the user of imminent layer shifts and recommends low-inductance motors (e.g., LDO Super Power), providing a direct affiliate link.3.2. Square Corner Velocity (SCV) vs. Jerk ConverterProblem StatementUsers migrating from legacy Marlin firmware to Klipper often struggle with the transition from "Jerk" to "Square Corner Velocity" (SCV). These two parameters control cornering behavior but are geometrically distinct. Inputting a Jerk value of 10 into Klipper's SCV setting results in violent motion because SCV values are mathematically smaller for the same kinematic effect.14Theoretical FrameworkMarlin Jerk: Defines the instantaneous change in velocity allowed ($V_{inst}$).Klipper SCV: Defines the velocity at which the toolhead travels through a 90° corner ($V_{90^\circ}$).The relationship is derived from the geometry of a 90° turn where the X and Y velocity components change. The conversion factor is $\sqrt{2}$.Mathematical ModelTo convert a familiar Marlin Jerk value to Klipper SCV:$$\text{SCV}_{Klipper} = \text{Jerk}_{Marlin} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx \text{Jerk} \cdot 0.707$$To convert a Klipper SCV to an equivalent Jerk (for comparison):$$\text{Jerk}_{Marlin} = \text{SCV}_{Klipper} \cdot \sqrt{2} \approx \text{SCV} \cdot 1.414$$Algorithm ImplementationInputs: Mode (Marlin to Klipper / Klipper to Marlin), Value.Output: The mathematically equivalent setting.Context: Include a tooltip explaining that SCV defaults of 5.0 mm/s are standard, and values above 10.0 mm/s typically require high-torque hardware to prevent corner ringing.4.
